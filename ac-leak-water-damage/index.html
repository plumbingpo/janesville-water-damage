Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Janesville

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

Measure the Spread

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Mold Near the Return

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Insulation Around the Unit

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.
